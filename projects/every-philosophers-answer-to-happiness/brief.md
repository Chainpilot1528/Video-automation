# Brief: Every Philosopher's Answer to Happiness

## Central question

What does it take to be happy? Five philosophers, five answers, each one a step deeper than the last.

## Format

About three minutes. Archival style: public-domain artworks on a black canvas, kinetic quotes, portrait medallions, and a chapter map of descending stone steps. Narrated with the Reckoner voice (`--preset reckoner --humanize`) over a quiet music bed.

Steps (levels of the map):
1. Epicurus: pleasure, rightly understood
2. Aristotle: flourishing over a whole life
3. Epictetus and the Stoics: what is up to us
4. Schopenhauer: the pendulum
5. John Stuart Mill: look away

## Audience and promise

Curious viewers who want the ideas, not a lecture. They leave with five usable answers and one twist: the deepest answer is to stop staring at happiness.

## Rules

- Every on-screen quote is a ledger quotation from a public-domain translation, matched word for word against the source text.
- Interpretations are said as interpretations ("for Aristotle", "on this view"), never as fact.
- Images are public-domain museum works, labelled with what they actually show.

## Known risks

- No public-domain portraits of Schopenhauer or Mill in The Met or the Art Institute of Chicago.
- The Stoics level shows a bust of Chrysippus (a Stoic), not Epictetus; it must be labelled as Chrysippus.
- Translator attribution of the Gutenberg Aristotle (Chase) needs a human check.
