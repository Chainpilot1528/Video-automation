# Brief: Why Your Brain Hates Contradictions

Source: the creator's own Substack essay (https://reckoningself.substack.com/p/why-your-brain-hates-contradictions).

## Central question

When reality disagrees with what we believe, why do we so often defend the belief, and how can we notice when we're doing it?

## Format

About 9-10 minutes, archival style (public-domain artworks on black, kinetic quotes, a stone-steps chapter map with one level per section group), narrated with Reckoner (`--preset reckoner --humanize`) as a person explaining, not reading. The script is a spoken adaptation of the essay, in its ten sections.

## Rules

- Research statements match the essay's references (Festinger & Carlsmith 1959, Anglin 2019, Veiga-Zarza et al. 2026), checked against the papers or their abstracts.
- On-screen quotations are the essay's own sentences, verbatim.
- Artworks are metaphors, captioned with what they really are; nothing pretends to show the people or studies discussed.
